The 14th Amendment

1868 Reconstruction 2026-07-02

After Dred Scott tried to deny Black citizenship, the 14th Amendment wrote citizenship into the Constitution.

Ratified in 1868, the amendment established birthright citizenship, due process, and equal protection. It was a direct Reconstruction answer to slavery and the Supreme Court's Dred Scott decision.

Its promises became central to civil rights lawsuits, school desegregation, voting rights, marriage equality, and debates over who counts as fully protected by law.

Why It Matters

The 14th Amendment matters because citizenship cannot depend on political mood. It is one of the strongest constitutional tools for challenging unequal treatment.
