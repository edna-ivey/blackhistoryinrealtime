Environmental Justice

20th Century — PresentEnvironmental Justice2026-04-22

Environmental justice names a reality Black communities have long lived with: pollution, toxic exposure, waste sites, unsafe water, and climate vulnerability are not distributed evenly. They follow power.

Factories, highways, landfills, and petrochemical corridors have often been placed near communities with the least political protection and the greatest racial marginalization.

The movement for environmental justice insists that environmental harm is also a civil rights issue, a health issue, and a question of whose lives are considered disposable.

Cost / Impact

Communities pay with higher asthma rates, cancer risk, water insecurity, displacement, and shortened life expectancy while receiving fewer protections and slower response.

Why It Matters

Environmental injustice is not accidental exposure. It is structured neglect. Naming it clearly is the first step toward changing it.
