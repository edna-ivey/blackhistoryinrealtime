Josephine Baker

1906 — 1975Culture & Resistance2026-04-23

Josephine Baker became internationally famous as a performer, but her public glamour concealed a much deeper political life. During World War II, she assisted the French Resistance and used her visibility in service of anti-fascist work.

She moved through elite spaces while gathering information, supporting the cause materially, and placing herself in genuine danger. Her celebrity gave her access. Her courage gave that access purpose.

Baker's life resists simplification. She was not just an icon. She was strategic.

Cost / Impact

Her political work placed her at risk, and her life was shaped by racism both in the United States and abroad, even as she became internationally celebrated.

Why It Matters

Josephine Baker reminds us that performance and resistance are not opposites. Black women have often had to move through both at once.
