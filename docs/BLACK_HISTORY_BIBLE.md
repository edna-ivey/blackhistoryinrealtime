**Status:** Editorial and encyclopedia source of truth

## Mission

Black History In Real Time publishes one new Black history story every day.
The format is a daily quiz linking to a full encyclopedia entry. Every entry
is written for ages 10–14: narrative first, curriculum never.

The site commits to telling Black history in full — starting before the
Atlantic slave trade with Pre-Colonial Africa and continuing to the present
day. The Subject Registry in this document tracks every subject across every
stage of that mission.

## Field Definitions

### BHIRT ID

Each subject has a permanent unique identifier in the format `BHIRT-NNNN`.
IDs never change. They are assigned alphabetically by subject name, ignoring
leading articles (The, A, An) and sorting numbers before letters.

Assignment rule: Sort all subjects ignoring leading "The", "A", or "An".
Numbers sort before letters. Leading articles are stripped for sort purposes
only — the displayed subject name retains the article.

### Theme

Single controlled value. Replaces compound labels. Assign the one value that
best describes what the entry is fundamentally about.

| Value | Definition | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Resistance | Active opposition to oppression through rebellion, escape, civil disobedience, or organized defiance | Not Justice (legal battles); not Freedom (the destination) — Resistance is the act of OPPOSING |
| Justice | Legal battles, court rulings, civil rights law, accountability for racial violence, and moral reckoning with injustice | Not Resistance (defiance); not Citizenship (civic participation) — Justice is what courts and laws did or failed to do |
| Education | Access to learning, intellectual traditions, scholarship, and the defense and expansion of Black knowledge | Not Witness (truth-telling); not Culture (creative works) — Education is about the institutions and practices of learning itself |
| Culture | Creative expression through music, literature, visual art, film, theater, dance, cuisine, and fashion | Not Identity (selfhood); not Witness (exposing truth) — Culture is the act of making art or cultural forms |
| Innovation | STEM achievements, inventions, medical discoveries, technical firsts, and pioneering methods | Not Wealth (economic innovation); not Culture (artistic innovation) — Innovation is technical and scientific advancement |
| Wealth | Economic power, wealth creation, financial exploitation, labor economics, and economic self-determination | Holds both sides — what was built (Black Wall Street) and what was taken (Enslaved Labor, Redlining) |
| Power | Political office, governing authority, political movement leadership, and the wielding or pursuit of institutional power | Not Resistance (opposing power); not Community (social infrastructure) — Power is about governance and political authority |
| Military | Military service, wartime contributions, combat history, and the contradiction of serving a nation that denied Black citizenship | A self-contained cluster built around service under exclusion |
| Faith | Religious community, spiritual leadership, and faith as an organizing, sustaining, and mobilizing force in Black life | Not reducible to Community or Power; the spiritual and religious dimension is distinct |
| Community | Institution building, mutual aid, collective organization, and structures that sustain Black community life outside political systems | Distinct from political Power and educational achievement |
| Identity | Selfhood, cultural expression of who Black people are, LGBTQ+ experience, linguistic identity, and the politics of representation | A cluster about BEING rather than DOING — selfhood, expression, and belonging |
| Systems | How structural mechanisms of racial oppression operated as policies, programs, or sustained institutional practices — the subject IS the system | Teaches structural analysis; students learn to see systems, not just events |
| Freedom | Emancipation, liberation, self-determination, diaspora, and the memory and meaning of what it means to be free | Distinct from Resistance (the act of fighting); Freedom is the destination and state |
| Witness | Documentary truth-telling through journalism, photography, testimony, or scholarship that exposed, preserved, or recovered hidden truth | Distinct from Culture (artistic creation) and Education (intellectual analysis) |
| Citizenship | Civic participation, voting rights, constitutional rights, and the ongoing battle to be recognized as a full citizen | Distinct from legal battles (Justice) and active resistance (Resistance) |

### Slug

The URL-safe identifier for this subject's encyclopedia page. Format: kebab-case,
lowercase, no articles (the/a/an) at start, derived from subject name. Assigned
when a page is generated or planned. TBD until confirmed.

Example: `carter-woodson`, `mansa-musa`, `james-meredith`

### Narrative Archetype

The single narrative role this subject plays in Black history. Governs story
structure and opening approach. Select one value. Required before Drafting.

| Value | Definition | Typically used for |
|---|---|---|
| Builder | Creates lasting institutions, communities, organizations, or infrastructure | Person — use when the lasting thing created is the story, not the organizing |
| Scholar | Advances knowledge, education, research, or historical understanding | Person — use when the defining contribution is ideas and intellectual frameworks |
| Trailblazer | First person or group to break a significant barrier | Person — use when "first" is the central fact; entering a space, not opposing it |
| Resister | Actively opposes oppression or injustice through direct action | Person — the defining act is opposing: escaping, defying, confronting |
| Leader | Organizes people toward a common goal | Person — use when the defining contribution is assembling and directing collective effort |
| Innovator | Creates new inventions, discoveries, or methods | Person — technical and scientific achievement |
| Artist | Shapes culture through music, literature, visual art, performance, or media | Person, Cultural — the contribution is a body of creative work |
| Witness | Documents, exposes, records, or reveals truth | Person — journalism, photography, testimony; distinct from Artist |
| Movement | Collective action by many people | Movement, Event — the subject IS the collective action |
| Institution | An enduring organization or structure | Institution — the subject IS the institution |
| System | A policy, mechanism, or structure that shapes society | System — the subject IS the structural system |
| Turning Point | An event that significantly changed the direction of history | Event — redirected what was legally or politically possible |

**Edge case rule:** Persons whose death became a historical turning point (George
Floyd, Emmett Till, Medgar Evers, Fred Hampton) use **Turning Point** even though
Content Type = Person. The story is about the moment, not their accumulated work.

### Reading Consideration

Editorial flag for entries that require special handling. Select one value.
Required before Drafting.

| Value | When to assign |
|---|---|
| Standard | No special handling needed; narrative flows naturally for ages 10–14 |
| Complex | Dense legal, economic, or institutional history; needs extra simplification |
| Sensitive | Contains graphic violence, explicit racism, trauma, or contested framing |

### Angle

A single sentence (in quotes) that captures the irreplaceable hook of this entry
— what makes this subject unforgettable to a 10–14 year old reader. Used to
distinguish repeated figures and guide the drafting process. Required before
Drafting. TBD until confirmed.

Example: `"The man who invented Black History Month because textbooks were erasing Black people"`

### Drafting Gate

A subject may not advance to Drafting workflow status until all 12 fields below
are assigned. TBD is not permitted in any of these fields at Drafting.

1. BHIRT ID
2. Subject
3. Slug
4. Content Type
5. Historical Era
6. Series
7. Significance Type
8. Narrative Archetype
9. Educational Objective
10. Audience Emotion
11. Reading Consideration
12. Angle

**Not in the Drafting Gate:** Theme and Tags (required before Publishing);
Importance Level (required at registration); Date(s) (not required for Pool
subjects).

## Related Subject Network

Encyclopedia pages link to related subjects via the `getConnections()`
function in `generated/encyclopedia-data.js`. That function currently scores
connections by shared tags. As the encyclopedia grows, the Related field in
each registry entry will improve connection quality beyond tag matching.

### Standard Relationship Model

| Category | Definition | Example (James Meredith) |
|---|---|---|
| Direct Connection | Shared event, organization, or cause | Freedom Riders, SNCC |
| Thematic Connection | Shared theme, era, or system | Ruby Bridges (education integration), John Lewis (courage & citizenship) |
| Contrast / Tension | Different strategy or outcome on the same issue | Malcolm X (self-determination vs. integration) |

## Duplication Prevention

### Rules

1. A subject may not appear on two calendar dates with the same angle, hook,
   quiz question, Wait...What?! moment, or Why It Matters section. See
   `content/writing-bible.md` for the Repeated Figure Rule.

2. Before proposing any new subject, check the Subject Registry. The registry
   is authoritative for all tracked subjects across all statuses.

3. Subjects already appearing in Published status are listed in the Locked
   Subjects section below.
