# BlackHistoryInRealTime Writing Bible

## Mission

Black history should feel alive.

Every entry should make readers feel like they are meeting a real person, not reading a textbook.

The goal is not to impress readers with knowledge.

The goal is to make readers care.

---

## Audience

**Primary Audience:**
- Ages 10–14

**Secondary Audience:**
- Parents
- Teachers
- Adults who never learned this history

Assume the reader knows very little.

Never assume historical knowledge.

---

## Reading Level

**Target:** 5th–8th grade

**Use:**
- Short paragraphs
- Simple vocabulary
- Clear explanations

**Avoid:**
- Academic language
- Long sentences
- Unexplained historical terms
- Writing that sounds like Wikipedia

---

## Opening Rule

**Never** start with:

> "John Smith was born..."

Instead start with:
- A surprising fact
- A question
- A dramatic moment
- A conflict
- A challenge

**Examples:**

> "Imagine being told you couldn't learn to fly because of your race and your gender."

> "At fifteen years old, she made a decision that changed American history."

> "Nobody expected him to become a hero that day."

---

## The Three Questions

Every biography must answer:

1. Why should I care?
2. What obstacle stood in their way?
3. What changed because of them?

If any of those questions are missing, the entry is incomplete.

---

## The Wait... What?! Rule

Every entry should contain at least one moment that makes readers stop and think:

> "Wait... What?!"

**Examples:**
- Bessie Coleman learning French and moving to France to become a pilot.
- Robert Smalls stealing a Confederate ship.
- Mamie Till-Mobley insisting on an open casket.

These moments should be highlighted naturally within the story.

---

## The Movie Scene Rule

Every entry must contain at least one moment that readers can visualize like a movie scene.

**Do not summarize the moment. Show the moment happening.**

**Bad:**
> "Robert Smalls escaped slavery by commandeering a Confederate ship."

**Good:**
> "Before sunrise, Robert Smalls put on a Confederate captain's hat, guided a stolen ship past armed guards, and sailed toward Union lines while his family hid below deck."

The reader should be able to picture the moment.

---

## Story Structure

- **Paragraph 1:** Hook
- **Paragraph 2:** Background
- **Paragraph 3:** Obstacle or conflict
- **Paragraph 4:** Turning point
- **Paragraph 5:** Impact and legacy

---

## Why It Matters Section

This section must connect the past to today.

**Avoid:**
> "This person was important."

**Instead answer:**
> "What would be different if they had never existed?"

---

## Tone

Curious.

Respectful.

Hopeful.

Human.

Never preachy. Never dry. Never lecture.

---

## What to Avoid

**Do not write like:**
> "She was an African-American educator, activist, and philanthropist."

**Write like:**
> "She believed every child deserved a chance, even when most people disagreed."

---

## Repeated Figure Rule

A person may appear more than once in BlackHistoryInRealTime only when:

- A different life stage is explored
- A different historical moment is explored
- A different lesson is taught
- A different audience takeaway exists

**Repeat entries must not reuse:**
- The same hook
- The same quiz
- The same "Wait... What?!" moment
- The same Why It Matters section

Each appearance should feel like a new chapter of that person's life.

---

## Event and System Entries

Not every entry should be a person.

Events, laws, systems, institutions, movements, and turning points should also be represented.

**Examples:**
- Juneteenth
- Reconstruction
- Black Codes
- The Freedmen's Bureau
- The 13th Amendment
- The 14th Amendment
- The 15th Amendment

These should follow the same storytelling standards as biographies.

An event entry should still answer the Three Questions. It should still have a hook, a "Wait... What?!" moment, and a Why It Matters that connects to today.

The subject of the story is the moment — not a person — but the moment should still feel human.
